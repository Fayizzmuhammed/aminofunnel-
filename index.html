Why it happens

Your muscles are being rebuilt every day. With age, the rebuild slows down.

Muscle isn't permanent. Your body is constantly breaking down old muscle protein and building new protein to replace it. In your 20s, the building side wins easily. After 40, it starts to fall behind — researchers call this gradual loss sarcopenia.

One big reason: as we age, our bodies get less efficient at turning the protein we eat into new muscle. You can eat "enough" protein on paper and still not give your muscles the specific building blocks they need.
